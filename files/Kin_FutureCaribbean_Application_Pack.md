## Section 2 — Track & solution (the core scored answers)

### Q22 — Track
**02 — Finance, Payments & MSME Capital**

### Q23 — Why did you choose this track?
We already operate in it. 360 SaaS builds products for Jamaica and the wider Caribbean — MioBP in digital health, Tropic Trade in commerce, and Kin Banking in financial services. Running real products in-market surfaced the problem underneath everything: the same consumers and micro-businesses who transact through our platforms every day are invisible to credit. They generate steady, legible cash flow and still cannot borrow, because lending here is collateral-based, not cash-flow-based. This track is about exactly that gap — moving capital efficiently and underwriting the underwritten — and we have live distribution, the data, and the regional focus to build infrastructure here rather than theorise about it.

### Q24 — What real-world opportunity are you trying to solve?
Across LATAM and the Caribbean, 200M+ adults are unbanked or underbanked, $100B+ in remittances flow in annually at 6–9% fees, and 80–90% of businesses are MSMEs that cannot borrow because lending is collateral-based. The capital exists; the coordination layer does not. Thin-file individuals and small traders generate steady cash flow — marketplace sales, remittances received, wallet transfers, POS takings, airtime and utility payments — but it is fragmented across systems and never becomes a credit decision. The opportunity is to turn that fragmented cash-flow data into (a) collateral-free working capital and (b) cheapest-route money movement, as shared infrastructure other institutions can plug into. We see this first-hand: Tropic Trade sellers and MioBP users are exactly the people the formal system leaves out.

### Q25 — Describe your proposed solution or idea
**Kin Capital Rails** — an agentic AI infrastructure layer with one orchestrator coordinating four specialised tool-agents:

1. **Data Unification Agent** — ingests fragmented inputs (Tropic Trade marketplace sales, remittance history, wallet/P2P transfers, merchant/POS activity, airtime/utility payments) and normalises them into one real-time cash-flow ledger per person or MSME.
2. **Cash-Flow Credit Agent** — underwrites from that ledger: income regularity, volatility, seasonality and remittance-dependency become an explainable, collateral-free credit profile, a risk score, and a recommended working-capital limit — with reasons attached.
3. **Remittance Routing Agent** — for any transfer, evaluates available rails (correspondent banks, settlement/stablecoin rails, internal Kin-to-Kin netting) and picks the cheapest, fastest path, driving fees from ~8% toward under 1%.
4. **Compliance / RegTech Agent** — runs KYC/AML screening and transaction monitoring across jurisdictions and writes a full, regulator-ready audit trail.

The **orchestrator** (built on OpenClaw / open-source LLM orchestration) runs the agentic loop — *perceive → reason → plan → act → reflect* — sequencing which agents to call, executing, checking its own output, and **escalating to a human** loan or compliance officer when confidence is low or an amount crosses a threshold. Outputs: an instant explainable credit offer to the user, a risk-scored decision **API/dashboard** lenders and NBFIs can fund against, cheapest-route transfer execution, and an audit trail. Our products are the front doors that generate the data; the Rails are the infrastructure other institutions integrate.

### Q26 — Why does this matter for the Caribbean or the world?
Fixing financial coordination here unlocks business growth, job creation, regional trade and stronger capital markets. Collateral-free, cash-flow-based credit means viable micro-businesses — including the Caribbean sellers building on Tropic Trade — can finally borrow. Kin-to-Kin netting and smart routing keep remittance value with families instead of intermediaries and keep capital circulating in-region. And the constraints that make this hard — multiple currencies, jurisdictions, languages, fragmented data, thin institutional rails — are the conditions of fragmented markets everywhere. If the underwriting and routing layer works across CARICOM, it transfers to other diaspora-heavy, thin-file economies in LatAm, West Africa and South Asia.

### Q27 — If your solution succeeds, what could it become in 5 years?
The default credit-and-capital rail for the Caribbean's unbanked — the underwriting layer banks, fintechs and NBFIs plug into to lend to thin-file individuals and MSMEs across the region, with in-region netting that materially cuts the cost of moving money. Our consumer and commerce products (MioBP, Tropic Trade, Kin Banking) become front-ends that feed and ride the Rails; the Rails become the infrastructure underneath them and beyond, then expand to other fragmented markets globally. The measure of success is simple: capital reaching businesses that today it never reaches.

## 4-person, 21-day build plan (17 July – 7 August)

The competition rewards a **deployable, working system** over a polished idea — "the goal is momentum." Scope is a thin but complete end-to-end slice, not all six track categories.

**Days 1–3 — Foundations & data.** Repo, environments, compute access, OpenClaw orchestrator skeleton. Assemble datasets: Findex + Remittance Prices; shape representative Tropic Trade marketplace data and a synthetic transaction generator. Lock the unified cash-flow ledger schema. *(Omar + Member 4 lead; Member 3 scaffolds the orchestrator; Camarlo defines decision rules.)*

**Days 4–9 — Core agents.** Data Unification Agent (fragmented inputs → one ledger) and Cash-Flow Credit Agent (scorecard + explainability) in parallel with the Remittance Routing Agent (rail/fee table, Kin-to-Kin netting, cheapest-path selection). *(Member 3 owns credit + reasoning; Member 4 owns ingestion; Omar owns routing + APIs.)*

**Days 10–14 — Compliance, orchestration, human-in-the-loop.** Compliance/RegTech Agent (KYC/AML, monitoring, audit trail). Wire the orchestrator loop end-to-end — plan, call tools, reflect, escalate. Build the loan-officer/compliance review UI and the lender dashboard; connect a product front-end. *(Whole team; Camarlo drives review UX and lender narrative.)*

**Days 15–18 — Integration, metrics, hardening.** One clean end-to-end flow: marketplace seller → unified ledger → explained credit offer → funded → remittance routed → compliance logged. Compute the headline numbers. Handle edge cases, failures, logging.

**Days 19–21 — Demo, docs, deploy.** Record the demo, write the README + architecture docs, deploy a hosted demo, finalise the diagram, prep submission. Build in public throughout (the event rewards sharing progress).

**Headline metrics to demonstrate (the "does it change outcomes?" bar):**
- Remittance fee reduced from ~8% to **<1%** on netted/matched routes (simulated corridors).
- **% of thin-file, zero-collateral users** (e.g. Tropic Trade sellers) who receive a viable working-capital offer under the cash-flow model vs the collateral model — the inclusion delta.
- Underwriting decision time: **minutes vs weeks.**
- Risk separation: the model meaningfully ranks risk on held-out representative data (report an AUC-style figure).
